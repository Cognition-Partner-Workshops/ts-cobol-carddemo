What the monthly interest run does

Once a month the job INTCALC charges interest on card accounts. It runs one program, CBACT04C, which reads the file of balances that the posting job keeps per account and per kind of transaction (purchases, cash advances, payments and so on).

For each of those balances the program looks up the yearly interest rate that applies to the account's disclosure group and to that kind of transaction. If the group has no rate of its own, the rate of the group called DEFAULT is used. A rate of zero means no interest. Otherwise one month of interest is worked out as balance x yearly rate / 1200 and cut off at the cent, and one interest transaction for that amount is written to a new transaction file, which a later job merges into the transaction history.

When the program moves on to the next account, it adds the interest of the account it just finished to that account's current balance and sets the account's cycle credit and cycle debit to zero.

Things a business owner should know before this is rebuilt: interest is cut off, not rounded; the last account on the file gets its interest transactions but its balance is never updated; a credit (negative) balance gets negative interest; the transaction id starts with a date typed into the job, and the timestamp is the moment the job ran; fees are announced in the job but not calculated; and nothing stops a second run from charging the same interest again.

QUIRK 6 behaviour that follows from how the code is written, not confirmed as policy

QUIRKINT-Q01

Interest is cut off at the cent, not rounded

UNDECIDED

The interest amount is truncated to two decimals towards zero, so 12.355 is charged as 12.35.

Why it is open: The COMPUTE statement has no ROUNDED phrase; nothing in the estate says truncation was chosen. docs/BASELINE.md:104-106 records it as behaviour to keep for parity, which is not a statement of policy.

Question for the business: Should monthly interest be cut off at the cent, or rounded?

  • A Keep as coded - truncate towards zero
  • B Round half up to the nearest cent
  • C Round half to even (banker's rounding)
